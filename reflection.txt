AP CSA Project Reflection: Turn-Based Tower Game

1. Standards Tracking
1) Primitive types: Player and Monster use double health and int attack/strength; Main uses boolean keepPlaying and double difficulty.
2) Arithmetic and casting: the Monster constructor casts (int) attack and scales exp by difficulty; Player.rest() uses +=; healthPercent() casts (double).
3) Custom classes: Player (two overloaded constructors) and Monster have private fields, getters, and setters, and are instantiated in Main.
4) Return methods: spawnMonsterForLevel(), getValidInt(), isAlive(), and healthPercent() results feed conditions and expressions.
5) Compound logic: the combat loop condition and combatChoice if/else-if chain in main().
6) Dynamic loops: while (keepPlaying) and the combat loop.
7) Count-based loop: for (int level = 1; level <= 5; level++).
8) Integration: break, continue, and level-- (fleeing) alter the level loop.

2. Design Trade-offs
Rather than hard-coding monster stats for each difficulty, I used one static multiplier, Main.difficulty. The Monster constructor scales health, attack, and EXP with it, while Player.rest() and trainHealth() scale healing inversely. This couples the classes, but difficulty can be tuned in one place.

3. Debugging Analysis
My hardest bug was a crash whenever a letter was typed at a menu. The stack trace pointed to a NumberFormatException from Integer.parseInt. I fixed it with getValidInt(), which wraps parseInt in try/catch inside a while(true) loop and re-prompts until the input is valid.

5. Design Trade-offs (Units 5–6)

I made weapons work automatically instead of having to “equip” them. Player.getAttack() returns strength plus totalWeaponPower(inventory), 
so any weapon in the Item[] array raises your attack. This kept the combat menu simple: only potions need a menu option (4. Drink Potion). 
It also makes fleeing matter, because losing a random item can lower your attack. The downside is that the player can’t choose which weapon 
to use, but with only 5 slots that didn’t seem necessary.

I also kept Analyze as an action that uses a turn, even though it was flagged in my Unit 1–4 feedback. In a turn-based battle,
information should cost something. Otherwise you would always Analyze first for free, and the option would stop being a choice.

6. Debugging Analysis (Units 5–6)

When I moved the monsters into a Monster[] floors array, fleeing broke. 
Fleeing sends you down a floor, but the monster on that floor was already defeated,
so the combat loop skipped straight to “Victory!” and gave free rest and training. 
Before, spawnMonsterForLevel() made a brand-new monster every time,
so this never happened. Now the same objects stay in the array and keep their health. 
I fixed it by checking !monster.isAlive() when entering a floor and resetting its health with setHealth(getMaxHealth()).
I also added a boolean[] cleared array so a revived monster doesn’t give EXP or item drops again, 
which stops players from farming lower floors.
